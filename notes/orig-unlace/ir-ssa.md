# Original unlace IR core (ssa, verify, memory_ssa, cfg): techniques vs main

Scope: `crates/unlace-ir/src/{ssa,verify,memory_ssa,cfg}.rs` on `origin/master` (the archived engine; blobs of 101 KB, 75 KB, 19 KB and 26 KB). Main status is checked on `origin/main` (188480126a). Read-only: `git show`, `rg` and `sed`; nothing was built or run.

Main's IR is a port of Ghidra's decompiler (`Funcdata`, heritage, block graph), so most of master's SSA core already has a counterpart. The real gaps are a whole-function verifier, type-level witnesses, a CFG dump, and the Go, Rust and C++ metadata that master attached to `SsaFunction`. Rows are ranked by value to port.

## Recommendation

Port rows 1, 2 and 3. Row 1 is the most useful, because blind-merge teams break invariants that only show up downstream. Row 2 changes printed output, so it goes behind an option. Row 3 is a small read-only dump. Rows 4 and 5 are worth a separate decision, depending on whether Go and Rust binaries are targets. Rows 6 to 9 are low value. Rows 10 to 16 are covered or deliberately out of scope.

## Checked and not found on main

- A standalone memory-SSA graph or clobber query (row 11).
- Any CFG DOT writer (row 3); the archived inventory's A5 says the same.
- Go defer, Rust vtable, Rust async, C++ variant and coroutine metadata (rows 4, 5, 7, 8, 9).
- Type-level witnesses (row 2).
