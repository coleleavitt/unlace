# Original unlace research techniques vs. current main

## Intro

This is a read-only comparison of the techniques the archived unlace research decompiler planned to use (the paper packets in `research/papers/`, `research/lvar/liverange/` and `research/formal/`, plus the design docs and code on `origin/master`) against current `main`. Nothing was built or run.

- Original side: `origin/master` (`3aaf121866`). The `research/` packets are local and untracked, so they were read from the checkout, not from git. Those packets name the planned techniques; the implementations were read from `origin/master` with `git show`.
- Main side: `main` checkout at `6dce295ca4`. Every "have" or "partial" below was checked with a grep or a file read at that commit.
- Out of scope, as asked: MCP, debugger, emulator, LSP, hand-written lifters. Rocq proofs stay on the watch list (see `notes/archived-master-inventory.md`, row 6).

Status key: **have** = main implements the technique (possibly by a different mechanism); **partial** = main covers a slice; **missing** = no equivalent on main.

## Notes on the ranking

- The top three are the ones that reach output quality directly and are missing on main. Items 1 and 2 are the cheapest to start.
- Item 4 (memory SSA) is the largest unported piece in the original's `unlace-ir`. Its status is partial on main, and the open memory-effects work overlaps it. It is ranked fourth on purpose.
- Items 5, 6, 11, 12 and 16 were on the original's planned list and are **have** on main, so no port is needed.
- Items 10 and 17 are out of scope for a port: the proofs are on the watch list, and the naming model is the agent.
